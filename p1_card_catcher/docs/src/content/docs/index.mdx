## The subject of this material

A Franka Panda hangs from a gantry above a table. A ball, a plate or a playing card is thrown at it. A sensor reports the object's position, with 3 mm of noise, 125 times a second, and loses one fix in ten. From those fixes alone the system must decide where and when the object will cross a plane in front of the arm, move the hand there in time, and close on it.

<figcaption>Three ball throws at one eighth of real time, wide view and close view. The predictor here is physics only; the planner is called 50 times a second.</figcaption>

Everything is simulated in MuJoCo 4.0 on the CPU, and three of its features carry weight here: **speculative contacts**, without which a card passes straight through a finger; the **ellipsoid fluid model**, which makes a card flutter; and **`mujoco.batch`**, which steps a few hundred worlds at once for evaluation and reinforcement learning.

The stack is deliberately hybrid. Each layer is a classical method where a classical method is exact, and a learned one only where the classical model has run out:

## The result in one paragraph

With a physics model that reproduces the simulator's integrator to $10^{-9}$ m, an extended Kalman filter predicts the ball's catch point to 0.35 cm RMS at 200 ms before the catch, and the arm catches 97.3 ± 0.3 % of 768 throws. The same filter is 6.6 cm off for the card. A recurrent network that learns only the residual acceleration, with a Gaussian likelihood, halves that to 3.2 cm, where a network given no physics reaches 5.1 cm; the card's catch rate rises from 10 % to 33 %, against a ceiling of 47 % that a perfect predictor would reach with this gripper. A suction disc retains the plate, which the gripper cannot hold at all, in 87 % of throws. For the ball, a small policy trained with PPO decides when to replan and keeps the catch rate of the 50 Hz baseline with one eighth of the planner calls. All figures are means over three seeds, and every one of them can be regenerated by the scripts listed in [Running it](/reference/running-it/).
