Bibliography

Manipulating plastic material

Section titled “Manipulating plastic material”

Shi, H., Xu, H., Huang, Z., Li, Y., & Wu, J. (2022). RoboCraft: Learning to See, Simulate, and Shape Elasto-Plastic Objects with Graph Networks. Robotics: Science and Systems. arXiv:2205.02909 The template for the graph-network model: particles, a graph network that predicts their displacement, and model-predictive control on a shape distance. Here the “particles” are the cage nodes of the flex, so they are the simulator’s own degrees of freedom and no perception step is needed.

Shi, H., Xu, H., Clarke, S., Li, Y., & Wu, J. (2023). RoboCook: Long-Horizon Elasto-Plastic Object Manipulation with Diverse Tools. Conference on Robot Learning. arXiv:2306.14447 Several tools and a planner that picks among them. The stretch target with three tools is the smallest version of that idea: search with every tool, execute the best prediction.

Huang, Z., Hu, Y., Du, T., Zhou, S., Su, H., Tenenbaum, J. B., & Gan, C. (2021). PlasticineLab: A Soft-Body Manipulation Benchmark with Differentiable Physics. International Conference on Learning Representations. arXiv:2104.03311 A benchmark for the same kind of task built on a differentiable material-point simulator. This project takes the other route: a non-differentiable finite-element simulator and sampling-based planning.

Learned simulators

Section titled “Learned simulators”

Sanchez-Gonzalez, A., Godwin, J., Pfaff, T., Ying, R., Leskovec, J., & Battaglia, P. (2020). Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks. International Conference on Machine Learning. arXiv:2002.09405 The encode-process-decode architecture used in the models, and the trick of adding noise to the inputs during training so that multi-step rollouts stay stable.

Ronneberger, O., Fischer, P., & Brox, T. (2015). U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation. MICCAI. arXiv:1505.04597 The architecture of the height-map baseline.

Johannink, T., et al. (2019). Residual Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control. International Conference on Robotics and Automation. arXiv:1812.03201 The general pattern behind the structured model: keep what a model gets right and learn only the difference.

Planning and uncertainty

Section titled “Planning and uncertainty”

Rubinstein, R. Y. (1999). The Cross-Entropy Method for Combinatorial and Continuous Optimization. Methodology and Computing in Applied Probability, 1, 127–190. The optimiser of the planner.

Williams, G., Wagener, N., Goldfain, B., Drews, P., Rehg, J. M., Boots, B., & Theodorou, E. A. (2017). Information Theoretic MPC for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning. International Conference on Robotics and Automation. MPPI, the other standard sampling-based planner. It weights all samples instead of keeping an elite set; with five parameters and a population of 32 the two behave alike, and the cross-entropy method was used.

Chua, K., Calandra, R., McAllister, R., & Levine, S. (2018). Deep Reinforcement Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models. NeurIPS. arXiv:1805.12114 Cross-entropy planning through an ensemble of learned dynamics models, the configuration of part VII before the oracle is added.

Lakshminarayanan, B., Pritzel, A., & Blundell, C. (2017). Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles. NeurIPS. arXiv:1612.01474 Independently initialised and trained copies of one network; their disagreement is the uncertainty signal tested as a trigger for asking the simulator, and found no better than chance for that purpose.

Mechanics

Section titled “Mechanics”

Simo, J. C., & Hughes, T. J. R. (1998). Computational Inelasticity. Springer. Return mapping for J2J_2J2​ plasticity, the algorithm behind the yield attribute described in the clay page.

Sederberg, T. W., & Parry, S. R. (1986). Free-Form Deformation of Solid Geometric Models. SIGGRAPH. A lattice of control points that deforms everything inside it by trilinear (or higher) interpolation: the idea behind MuJoCo’s dof="trilinear" flex.
