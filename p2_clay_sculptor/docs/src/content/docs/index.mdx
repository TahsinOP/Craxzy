## The subject of this material

A Franka Panda stands in front of a block of clay, 140 mm square and 40 mm thick, stuck to a board. Bolted to its flange is one of three tools: a flat press, a roller or a cross-shaped stamp. The arm is given a target shape, as a height map, and must deform the clay into it one action at a time.

<figure class="sim">
<video src="/video/oracle.mp4" loop muted playsinline controls preload="metadata"></video>
<figcaption>The three targets, real time, wide view and close view: a slab flattened with the roller, a single pocket with the press, an X with the stamp. Every action was chosen by the planner with the simulator itself as its model.</figcaption>
</figure>

The clay is an elasto-plastic solid, a feature that is new in MuJoCo 4.0: strain it beyond a limit and the deformation stays. Three more features of 4.0 carry weight: the implicit **`discrete` integrator** that keeps a soft solid stable at 5 ms steps, **`mjSTATE_PLASTIC`**, which makes the plastic strain part of a state that can be saved and restored exactly, and **`mujoco.batch`**, which runs dozens of saved states forward in parallel on the CPU.

The stack is hybrid in the same sense at every level: a derived model where one is exact, a learned one where the derived model has run out, and a rule for telling the two situations apart.
